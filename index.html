How Lost & Found works

Every concept, end to end — how an item is reported, matched, claimed, verified, and handed to its real owner. No item is handed over just because someone asks for it.

The key concepts

● LOST

Lost report

Filed by the owner who lost something. It records what it is, where and when it was last seen, and a description — so it can be matched against found items.

● FOUND

Found report

Filed by the person who found something that isn’t theirs. The finder keeps it safe or leaves it at a desk (library, security, hostel office) and says where it is held.

Listing

Public listing

What everyone can see: name, photo, category, location and date. Private contacts and secret identifying details are never shown on a public listing.

Match

Auto-match

The system compares every lost report with every open found report — same category, same location, shared keywords and close dates — and flags likely pairs on the Auto-matches page.

Claim

Claim

The way a person says “that’s mine.” A claim is not proof by itself — it starts verification. Anyone can claim, but only the real owner can pass the proof check.

Secret proof

Secret identifying detail

A detail only the real owner would know and that was never published: the lock-screen photo on a phone, a name written inside a notebook, a scratch, a sticker, what else was inside a wallet. You give it privately when you claim.

Verification

Admin verification

An admin compares your secret proof against the actual item and its description. If it fits, the claim is approved (Verified). If it doesn’t, it is rejected — and another claimant can still be checked.

Contact

Contacting the owner

Contact is arranged by the admin, not by posting phone numbers publicly. See the step-by-step below — this is how the original owner is reached safely in both directions.

Handover

Handover & return

The physical return: both people meet at a known desk, the owner shows college ID, the admin or desk confirms the item, and only then is the case marked Returned.

If you lost something

  1. 1
    Search firstCheck Browse and Auto-matches — it may already have been found and listed.
  2. 2
    Report it as LostGive the name, category, place, date and description. Keep one identifying detail out of the public description — you’ll need it as your secret proof.
  3. 3
    Claim the found listingWhen a match appears, open it and submit a claim with your secret detail and your contact. That contact goes only to the admin.
  4. 4
    Wait for verificationTrack it in My desk: Pending → Approved (Verified) or Rejected. If rejected, you can add a better proof detail and claim again.
  5. 5
    Collect it in personAfter approval, meet at the desk named for that item, show your college ID, check the item, and the case is marked Returned.

If you found something

  1. 1
    Keep it safe — or hand it to a deskFor valuables (wallet, phone, ID, keys), leave it at the security / library / hostel desk and note that place in your report.
  2. 2
    Report it as FoundDescribe it honestly, but withhold one identifying detail (amount of cash, card name, wallpaper) — that withheld detail is what the real owner must match.
  3. 3
    Let the system match itAuto-match links your listing to the owner’s lost report. You don’t need to hunt for the owner yourself.
  4. 4
    Don’t hand it to anyone directlyIf someone says it’s theirs, point them to claim it here. Direct handover without verification is how items go to the wrong person.
  5. 5
    Hand over at the desk, after approvalOnce the admin marks the claim Verified, hand the item over at the desk in the owner’s presence (or leave it at that desk for collection) and the case becomes Returned.

How the original owner is contacted

Phone numbers and emails are collected on reports and claims, but they are kept private — a listing only shows a public place like “Security desk, Main Block” when the item is held there. Contact then works like this:

  1. 1
    A match or a claim connects the two reportsEither auto-match pairs a lost and a found report, or the owner claims a found listing directly.
  2. 2
    The admin sees both private contactsThe reporter’s contact (from the report) and the claimant’s contact (from the claim) are visible only to the claimant themselves and to admins — never on the public page.
  3. 3
    The admin contacts both sidesUsing those private contacts (the email / phone each person gave), the admin tells the owner their claim result and tells the finder or desk that a verified owner is coming.
  4. 4
    After approval, the collection point is revealed in My deskThe verified owner sees where to collect the item in My desk; the reporter sees the claim marked Approved on their own report. That is the moment contact details are shared — after proof, not before.
  5. 5
    They meet at a known place, not privatelyCollection is at the library, security or hostel desk named for the item, during the day, with ID — safer for both sides than exchanging personal numbers publicly.
Status changes are the notification inside this app: watch My desk — a claim moving to Approved means “come and collect”, and a report moving to Claimed means someone is being verified for your item.

How we make sure it goes to the right owner

  • Three checks, all must pass: (1) the secret detail matches the real item, (2) the description / photo fits what was reported lost, and (3) the person collecting shows college ID that fits the claim.
  • One approved claim wins: once a claim is approved the item is Verified for that person. Other pending claims on the same item are not handed the item.
  • Two people claiming the same item? The admin compares both proofs against the item itself — the person whose secret detail matches the withheld detail is the owner. A guess or a public detail (colour, brand) is not enough.
  • False claims are rejected and stay on record against that claim, so the same person can’t keep guessing details until one fits.
  • Nothing is final until collection: Approved ≠ Returned. Only after the in-person ID check and handover does the admin mark it Returned and close the case.

What each status means

Open

Reported and still unmatched / unclaimed. Anyone can claim it.

Claimed

At least one claim is waiting for the admin to check its proof.

Verified

A claim passed verification. That claimant is the confirmed owner — collection is next.

Returned

Handed over in person after the ID check. The case is complete.

Closed

Closed without a return — for example a duplicate report, or an item no one claimed after being kept at the desk.

Common questions

What if there is no match for my lost item?

Keep the lost report open and check Auto-matches again over the next few days — a found report may be filed later, and matching runs every time a new item is added. Also check the physical desks (library, security, canteen) — not everything found gets reported online straight away.

What if I can’t give a secret detail?

Give the best detail you have — where you bought it, a mark or sticker, what was with it, the exact place and time you lost it. The admin weighs all of it, but a claim with no checkable detail may be held until a stronger claim is ruled out.

Can the finder keep an item nobody claims?

Not through this system. Unclaimed items stay at the desk and the case is eventually Closed as unclaimed — the finder doesn’t gain ownership by reporting it, and handing it back is always the default.

Why isn’t the reporter’s phone number shown so I can call them?

Because public numbers get misused and let anyone bypass verification. The admin-mediated route is slower by one step but is what stops the wrong person collecting your item — after your claim is approved, the collection contact is revealed to you in My desk.

I found a phone / wallet with ID inside — should I contact the owner myself?

Report it here and leave it at a desk instead. If the ID clearly names the owner, the admin uses that plus the private contacts to reach them — you should not open a wallet or unlock a phone looking for contact details.

ADSA

Data structures & algorithms

  • HashMap — items indexed by ID and by category (Map<String, Item>) for O(1) lookup instead of scanning the whole list.
  • ArrayList — the live item list that search, filter and sort run on.
  • Linear search — keyword search across title + description.
  • Sorting — by date, name and location (the Sort dropdown on Browse).
  • Matching algorithm — weighted scoring of lost × found pairs.

OOP

Object-oriented design

  • Item is the parent class; LostItem and FoundItem inherit from it.
  • User is the parent; Admin inherits and adds verify / close powers.
  • Claim links a User to an Item with proof of ownership.
  • Encapsulation: fields are private, access is through getters/setters. Polymorphism: getStatusLabel() behaves differently for lost vs found.
class Item { String id, title,
  category, location, status; }

class LostItem extends Item {
  String lastSeenAt; }
class FoundItem extends Item {
  String heldAt; }

class User { String uid, name,
  email, role; }
class Admin extends User {
  boolean verifyClaim(Claim c) {} }
DBMS

Database design

  • Four tables: Users, Items, Claims, Locations.
  • Primary keys: user_id, item_id, claim_id, location_id.
  • Foreign keys: Items.reported_by → Users, Claims.item_id → Items, Claims.claimant_id → Users, Items.location_id → Locations.
  • Normalised to 3NF — location names live once in Locations, not repeated as free text in every item.
SELECT i.title, l.name, c.status
FROM Items i
JOIN Locations l
  ON i.location_id = l.location_id
LEFT JOIN Claims c
  ON c.item_id = i.item_id
WHERE i.status = 'open';

Likely viva questions

Why a HashMap and not only an ArrayList?

An ArrayList search is O(n) — you check every item. A HashMap keyed by item ID or category gives average O(1) lookup. This app keeps both: the ArrayList for ordered browsing and sorting, the HashMap-style index for instant lookup by ID/category.

Where is inheritance used?

Item is the base class; LostItem adds “last seen” details and FoundItem adds “where it is held”. User is the base class; Admin extends it with verifyClaim() and closeCase(). Shared fields live once in the parent.

How is the database normalised?

Locations are a separate table, so a location name is stored once and referenced by foreign key — no update anomaly if “Main Library” is renamed. Claims are separate from Items, so one item can have many claims without repeating item data. That is 3NF.

How does auto-match work?

For each lost item, every open found item is scored: same category +40, same location +30, shared keywords up to +20, dates within 3 days +10. Pairs scoring 50+ are shown as strong matches on the Auto-matches page.

How do you stop the wrong person claiming an item?

The claimant must give a secret identifying detail that is never shown publicly — for example the wallpaper on a phone, or a name written inside a notebook. Only an admin sees it, compares it, and approves or rejects the claim.
